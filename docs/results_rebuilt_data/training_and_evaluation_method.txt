Training and evaluation on the rebuilt dataset
==============================================

Model: model_rebuilt_data.h5
Seed: 202 (separate from split seed 4 and augmentation seed 101)


WHICH ARCHITECTURE, AND WHY IT NEEDED DECIDING
----------------------------------------------
The deployed model file, model2811_36_21_d130.h5, contains two
convolutional layers (32, 64) and 51,434,058 parameters.

Its filename encodes a naming scheme used during the original project:
2811 = 28 November, 361 = three conv layers 32/64/128, 21 = two dense
layers 256/128, d130 = trained on the 130-image dataset, GOOD = a
result worth keeping.

The filename therefore describes a three-conv model, but the file
contains two. The Flatten output of 200,704 (56 x 56 x 64) confirms
this - a third conv layer would give 100,352 and roughly half the
parameters.

The training code in final_data_ready.ipynb defines the three-conv
model the filename describes. So the surviving training code and the
deployed model file are different networks, and the metrics recorded
in that notebook come from the three-conv model, not the deployed one.

The rebuild retrains the TWO-conv architecture, because that is what
the deployed system loads and what Track A measured. Comparing across
different architectures would confound the split with the model.

The two-conv architecture was reconstructed from the .h5 metadata
rather than copied from the notebook. Parameter count verified at
51,434,058 before training - an exact match to the deployed file.


CONFIGURATION
-------------
  Conv2D(32, 3x3, relu, padding=same)   -> MaxPool 2x2 stride 2
  Conv2D(64, 3x3, relu, padding=same)   -> MaxPool 2x2 stride 2
  Flatten -> Dense(256, tanh) -> Dense(128, tanh) -> Dense(10, softmax)

  Input 224x224x3, rescale 1/255
  Adam, learning rate 0.0001
  categorical_crossentropy
  batch size 16, 15 epochs

All values match the original run. Nothing was tuned. The purpose of
this stage is to isolate the effect of the split, so every other
variable is held fixed.


TRAINING BEHAVIOUR
------------------
  Epoch  1: train 0.0900   val 0.1350
  Epoch  4: train 0.8211   val 0.5600
  Epoch  7: train 0.9989   val 0.6850
  Epoch  9: train 1.0000   val 0.7000
  Epoch 11: train 1.0000   val 0.7400   (lowest val loss, 0.8807)
  Epoch 15: train 1.0000   val 0.7350

Training accuracy reaches 1.0000 by epoch 9 and training loss
continues falling to 0.0085, while validation accuracy flattens around
0.70-0.74 from epoch 7 onward. After epoch 7 the model is refining its
fit to the 42 training sessions without learning anything that
transfers to unseen ones.

Validation loss reaches its minimum at epoch 11. Under early stopping
those weights would have been selected. The original used a fixed 15
epochs, and that was kept for comparability.

For reference, the same architecture on the original split reached
validation accuracy of approximately 0.98. The 24-point difference is
the effect of removing session overlap between training and
validation.


EVALUATION: TWO PASSES
----------------------
Both passes use the same trained model.

  Full test set  - 20 images per class, 200 total
                   9 real recordings + 11 augmented per class
                   Directly comparable to the original dataset's test
                   set, which was also 20 per class.

  Real only      - 9 images per class, 90 total
                   The real recordings alone, no augmentation.
                   Every image is an independent recording the model
                   has never encountered in any form.

The real-only figure is the primary measurement. It is the cleanest
estimate of generalisation the project can produce. Its limitation is
sample size: with 9 images per class, one image is 11 percentage
points of that class's accuracy.

The full-test figure exists for comparison with the original and is
slightly lower, as expected - augmented images are harder than clean
ones.


RESULTS
-------
  Full test set (200 images)
    loss 0.8225   accuracy 0.7150   macro-F1 0.7130

  Real recordings only (90 images)
    loss 0.6907   accuracy 0.7556   macro-F1 0.7537

Both were verified by two independent routes: model.evaluate() and a
manual predict-then-compare pass. The two agreed exactly in each case,
confirming that predictions and labels are correctly aligned.

This alignment check exists because the original evaluation code
omitted shuffle=False on the test generator, so predictions and labels
were compared in different orders. See audit_notes.md section 3.2.


CONFUSION PATTERNS
------------------
Four confusions appear in both passes, which is what makes them worth
reporting rather than treating as noise.

  bat <-> pen    bidirectional. 4 and 6 in the full test, 2 and 2 in
                 real-only.
  milk -> pen    one-directional. 7 and 3.
  red -> eat     one-directional and the largest single confusion.
                 10 of 20 in the full test, 4 of 9 in real-only.
                 eat is never mistaken for red.
  cup -> jump    5 and 2.

bat, pen and milk all begin with a bilabial closure - the lips pressed
together. The visual gesture is close to identical across them; what
distinguishes the words is largely in the sound. This is a known
limitation of visual-only speech recognition rather than a defect in
this model.

red -> eat has no equally clear explanation. It is the largest error
in both passes and is reported as an open observation.

Strongest classes: fish (F1 0.9000 real-only), drop and hot (0.8889).
Weakest: red (0.5882), pen (0.6000), bat (0.6250).

The asymmetry in eat's scores - recall 0.8889, precision 0.6667 - is
this pattern showing through: eat catches nearly all real eat images
but also absorbs a large share of red.
