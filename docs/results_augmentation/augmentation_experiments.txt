Augmentation experiments - results
==================================

Ten dataset configurations testing whether augmentation composition or
augmentation volume affects generalisation. Baseline architecture, five
seeds each, validation only.

Outcome: no configuration is reliably better than the original.
Several intermediate findings are worth recording, including one
apparent effect that a later configuration exposed as noise.


WHAT WAS BEING TESTED
---------------------
Every earlier stage varied how the data is processed - architecture,
regularisation, colour, geometry. None changed how much distinct
material the training set contains.

Two hypotheses remained:

  COMPOSITION  The 16 augmentation types were chosen during the
               original project without measurement. Four of them
               barely alter the image. Replacing them with types
               targeting variation the dataset actually contains
               might help.

  VOLUME       The original dataset holds 48 augmented training sets.
               More augmented sets means more training images from
               the same 42 recording sessions.


MEASURING THE CANDIDATES FIRST
------------------------------
Before selecting replacements, every candidate was measured the same
way the original 16 were: applied to a reference frame, mean absolute
pixel change recorded, and applied twice to establish whether it is
stochastic.

New frame-level candidates:

  RandomShadow        29.37
  CLAHEVarying        22.53
  GridDistortion      10.52
  ElasticTransform     4.27
  CoarseDropout        3.93
  MotionBlur           2.90
  ISONoise             1.43

New sequence-level candidates, which transform all 60 frames together:

                    mean change   frames altered (of 60)
  RateVariation         6.94              58
  SmoothDrift           4.04              46
  DropRefill            3.67              55
  FrameMorph            2.62              58

RateVariation was dropped: the measurement showed it deterministic -
the implementation contained no random draw - and it overlapped with
FrameMorph and DropRefill.

MotionBlur and ISONoise were dropped on the measurement. Both had been
proposed as improvements on GaussianBlur (2.49) and GaussNoise (10.52).
MotionBlur is barely different from what it would replace, and ISONoise
is seven times weaker than GaussNoise. The theoretical case for both
collapsed once measured.

RandomShadow had been assessed beforehand as weak. It measured 29.37,
stronger than most of the original 16. That assessment was wrong.


THE FOUR WEAKEST ORIGINAL TYPES
-------------------------------
  TemporalShift       0.00   a no-op; the branch returns the input
  OpticalDistortion   2.59   simulates lens distortion, but the camera
                             never changes between recordings
  GaussianBlur        2.49   focus loss; kernel 7-15 on an 80px image
                             should be dramatic, so the low figure
                             suggests it is barely applying
  Downsample          2.08   resolution loss, but all recordings are
                             720x1280

These four were replaced in both composition variants.

    I    original 16                234            20
    J    original 16                330            20

  B adds FrameMorph, DropRefill, SmoothDrift, CoarseDropout
  C adds RandomShadow, CLAHEVarying, GridDistortion, ElasticTransform

E, F and G evaluate on 25 images per class rather than 20. With 16
augmentation types and only 11 augmented evaluation slots, the standard
design cannot include every type in validation and test - five are
always absent. Raising to 16 augmented slots gives one of each. This
makes the evaluation more representative of the training distribution,
but the resulting figures are not comparable to the other groups.

COMPOSITION: NO EFFECT AT EQUAL VOLUME
--------------------------------------
B (0.716) and C (0.705) against A (0.718). Neither swap of four types
changed anything measurable.

This was predicted: replacing 4 of 16 types alters 25% of the
augmentation but only about 6% of the training images, and the
expected effect was below what 200 validation images can resolve.


COMPOSITION: A DIFFERENCE AT HIGHER VOLUME
------------------------------------------
Among E, F and G, temporal augmentation clearly beat photometric:

  F (temporal)   0.736   range 0.720-0.764
  G (photogeo)   0.690   range 0.680-0.696

4.6 points apart with no overlap across five seeds.

This is the reverse of what the magnitude measurement predicted. C's
replacements measured 29.37, 22.53, 10.52 and 4.27; B's measured 2.62,
3.67, 4.04 and 3.93. The weaker set performed better.

The finding: mean pixel change does not predict usefulness. A large
uniform photometric shift changes many pixels but teaches the model
little. A small change applied across a sequence in a temporally
structured way - where the alteration at frame 30 relates to the mouth
position at frame 31 - appears to carry more information.

This should be held loosely. B (temporal at 90 images per word) showed
no advantage over A, while F (temporal at 138) beat E clearly. Why
temporal augmentation would help only at the higher volume is not
explained, and at these sample sizes the F-vs-G gap could still be a
favourable draw.


VOLUME: AN APPARENT EFFECT THAT DID NOT SURVIVE
-----------------------------------------------
D at 138 images per word gave 0.758 against A's 0.718 - four points,
with ranges that did not overlap at all. D's worst seed equalled A's
best. Across every configuration tested in this phase, that had not
happened before, and it was recorded at the time as the first genuine
movement.

The ladder was then extended to test whether more helps further:

  A     90 images/word    0.718
  D    138                0.758
  H    186                0.733
  I    234                0.757
  J    330                0.744

Not monotonic. If volume drove the result, H would sit between D and I.
It sits below both, closer to A.

All four volume configurations fall between 0.733 and 0.758, with
overlapping ranges. The most likely reading is that D's separation from
A was a favourable draw, and H is the counter-example that exposes it.

There may be a weak positive tendency - all four exceed A - but the
ordering is wrong for a volume effect and the spread within the group
is as large as the gap from A.

This is worth recording as a methodological point as much as a result.
Five seeds with non-overlapping ranges felt like strong evidence at the
time. It was not sufficient. Only running the same design at a
different volume revealed the problem.


TRAINING ACCURACY
-----------------
1.0000 in every configuration, including J at 3,300 training images -
3.7 times A's volume.

This has now held across every working configuration in all four stages
of this phase, more than forty in total.

It is the clearest statement of the underlying limitation. Augmented
copies of 42 recording sessions do not add information regardless of
how many are produced. The model is not short of images; it is short of
recordings.


WHICH DATASET WAS KEPT
----------------------
A, the original.

Not because it performed best - nothing performed measurably best - but
because the Phase 2 test figures (71.50% full, 75.56% real-only) were
measured on it, and switching would require spending a test-set
evaluation to obtain a comparable number for a change the evidence does
not support.

D and I scored highest, but H shares D's design at higher volume and
scores 0.733. Selecting the top of that band would be selecting on
noise.

Worth noting for any future work: the full-coverage design used in E, F
and G is methodologically better than the standard one, independent of
its accuracy. A test set that excludes five of sixteen augmentation
types is evaluating on a narrower distribution than the model was
trained on. F specifically - full coverage with temporal augmentation -
has the best combination of design rationale and measured performance
of anything tested here, and would be the configuration to revisit if
this work continues.


AN IMPLEMENTATION NOTE
----------------------
Two assignment algorithms are used to build the augmentation plans.

The original method shuffles the type list and retries until no session
receives the same type twice. This works up to six uses per type.

At nine or more uses - groups H, I and J - most sessions must receive
seven or more distinct types from sixteen, and the probability that a
random shuffle satisfies every session at once becomes negligible. Five
hundred attempts all failed.

The higher-volume plans therefore use a constructive method that deals
types out in rounds, one full pass of the type pool per round, choosing
the session with the most remaining slots that has not yet received
that type.

The dispatch is on uses per type, six or fewer taking the original
path, so the earlier plans regenerate identically.
