Architecture experiments - results
==================================

Twelve configurations across three rounds, selected on validation,
confirmed once on test.

Outcome: the selected architecture performed WORSE on test than the
baseline it was meant to improve. This document records what was
tried, what was learned, and why the selection failed.


BASELINE
--------
2 conv blocks (32, 64), Flatten, Dense(256, tanh), Dense(128, tanh),
Dense(10, softmax). 51,434,058 parameters.

  Validation (3 seeds): mean 0.7167, range 0.7000-0.7250
  Full test (200):      0.7150
  Real only (90):       0.7556


ROUND 1 - depth and pooling head (4 configs, 3 seeds)
-----------------------------------------------------
  Config                  Params      Params/sample   Mean val
  c0  2 blocks, Flatten   51,434,058     57,149        0.7167
  c1  5 blocks, Flatten    8,025,546      8,917        0.7483
  c2  2 blocks, GAP           70,218         78        0.1200
  c3  5 blocks, GAP        1,734,090      1,927        0.4033

c2 collapsed to chance level. Training accuracy was also at chance, so
it never learned rather than overfitting. Two conv blocks produce
edge-level channels; averaging 64 of them across the whole image
discards the spatial arrangement that carries the information.

c3 also failed badly, at 0.4033 with training accuracy 0.487 -
underfitting.

The hypothesis formed at this point was that global average pooling
destroys positional information, and that in a grid image position IS
time: cell (0,0) is frame 1, cell (9,5) is frame 60. Averaging
collapses all 60 frames into channel means, destroying the ordering.

Round 2 tested this and refuted it. See below.

c1's spread was 0.6900-0.8050 - 11.5 points across three seeds, versus
the baseline's 2.5. Its 3.2-point mean advantage was not distinguishable
from that spread.


ROUND 2 - position-preserving capacity reduction (5 configs, 5 seeds)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Config                       Params     Mean val   Std     Range
  e2  5 blocks + extra pool   2,782,666    0.743    0.024  0.725-0.785
  e0  5 blocks                8,025,546    0.733    0.034  0.700-0.790
  e3  6 blocks                5,142,474    0.728    0.023  0.705-0.755
  e4  4 blocks               13,267,914    0.688    0.012  0.675-0.700
  e1  5 blocks + 1x1 conv 32  2,020,842    0.206    0.220  0.100-0.600

e1 was the direct test of the position hypothesis. A 1x1 convolution
reduces 512 channels to 32 while preserving all 49 spatial positions.
If position were what GAP destroyed, e1 should have performed well.

It did not. Four of five seeds sat at chance with training accuracy
0.09-0.10 - never learned at all. One seed reached 0.60. That is a
training failure, not a capacity limitation, and it looks like an
initialisation lottery.

At the time this was read as refuting the position explanation, and
that reading was wrong. See the correction at the end of this
document.

What c2, c3 and e1 share is a narrow bottleneck immediately before tanh
dense layers, and that - not the loss of spatial position - is what
sank e1.

Other observations:

  Depth is not monotonic. 2 blocks 0.717, 4 blocks 0.688, 5 blocks
  0.733-0.743, 6 blocks 0.728.

  e4 (4 blocks) has the most parameters of any config at 13.3M,
  the highest training accuracy at 1.0000, and the worst validation
  of the working configs. More parameters produced more memorisation
  and worse generalisation.

  Parameter count alone does not predict performance in either
  direction.

Note on depth and parameters: deeper reduces parameters here, because
the feature map halves in each dimension faster than channels double.
4 blocks gives 13.3M; 5 gives 8.0M; 6 gives 5.1M.


ROUND 3 - dense activation (3 configs, 5 seeds)
-----------------------------------------------
  Config                 Params      Mean val   Std     Mean train
  f0  e2 with tanh      2,782,666     0.745    0.026     0.986
  f1  e2 with relu      2,782,666     0.660    0.031     0.914
  f2  baseline + relu  51,434,058     0.694    0.034     0.997

tanh beat relu by 8.5 points at 5 blocks, with identical parameter
counts and non-overlapping ranges (0.715-0.780 against 0.625-0.695).
This is the largest and cleanest effect found in the whole search.

f1's training accuracy is also lower - relu fits less, not more. That
is consistent with dead units: relu outputs zero for negative inputs
and those units stop receiving gradient permanently. In a dense layer
following a bottleneck, losing part of 256 units costs real capacity.

f2 tested the original 2-block architecture with relu, which the
project author recalled having failed during the original work. It
gave 0.694 against tanh's 0.717 - worse, though not the catastrophic
failure remembered. The tanh choice, made on a supervisor's
recommendation under time pressure, was correct.


FINAL EVALUATION - and the failure
----------------------------------
Selected config: 5 conv blocks, extra pooling, Flatten, tanh dense
layers. 2,782,666 parameters. Retrained with 3 seeds and evaluated on
test for the first time.

                     Baseline    Selected (mean of 3)
  Validation          0.7167          0.7450
  Full test (200)     0.7150          0.6750   (0.6700-0.6800)
  Real only (90)      0.7556          0.7148   (0.7000-0.7222)

Validation improved by 2.8 points. Test fell by 4 points on both
measures. The test range across seeds is tight, so this is not seed
noise.

WHY THIS HAPPENED

Twelve configurations were evaluated against the same 200 validation
images. Selecting the best of twelve will find configurations suited
to those particular 200 images, and part of that advantage does not
transfer. A 2.8-point gain is well within what selection pressure
alone can manufacture from twelve attempts.

This is the same category of error the wider reconstruction exists to
correct: measuring on data that has effectively been optimised
against. The rule "select on validation, touch test once" was set in
advance and followed. It was not sufficient, because it does not
address the size of the selection pool relative to the validation
set's resolution.

A second factor: validation and test are 9 recording sessions each,
differing in room, lighting and head angle. A configuration suited to
validation's 9 sessions need not suit test's 9. At this size,
validation is a weak proxy for test.


WHAT HOLDS UP
-------------
  - GAP and 1x1 bottlenecks fail badly with this architecture.
    Large, reproducible effects, not marginal.
  - tanh clearly beats relu at 5 blocks. 8.5 points, non-overlapping
    ranges across 5 seeds.
  - Depth beyond 2 blocks does not reliably improve test performance.
  - The 51M-parameter model can be replaced by a 2.8M model at
    roughly comparable test accuracy. An efficiency result, not an
    accuracy one.

WHAT DOES NOT HOLD UP
---------------------
  - The claim that the selected architecture is better. On test, it
    is not.
  -   - The claim that the selected architecture is better. On test, it
    is not.

(The GAP hypothesis was listed here as refuted. It has since been
confirmed - see the correction below.)


DECISION
--------
The baseline architecture is retained as the reference for subsequent
work. Stage 1 is recorded as a negative result.

Running further architecture searches now would compound the selection
problem rather than solve it. Later stages test data and training
changes, which are less exposed to this failure mode - but the same
constraint applies: few experiments, large expected effects, and the
understanding that a 200-image validation set cannot resolve small
differences.


CORRECTION - THE GAP HYPOTHESIS WAS CORRECT
===========================================
Added after the pretrained backbone experiments.

Round 1 of this stage produced the hypothesis that global average
pooling fails on grid images because it destroys positional
information: in a mosaic, spatial position IS frame index, so averaging
the feature map collapses all 60 frames into channel means.

Round 2 tested this with e1, a 1x1 convolution bottleneck that
preserves all 49 spatial positions while reducing channels. e1 failed -
four of five seeds never trained. This was taken as refuting the
hypothesis, and it was recorded above as not holding up.

That conclusion was wrong.

The pretrained backbone experiments settled it directly. Using a frozen
MobileNetV2 with identical weights and identical features, changing
only the head:

  GAP head       mean validation 0.310   training accuracy 0.297
  Flatten head   mean validation 0.618   training accuracy 0.999

The features were identical in both cases. The only difference was
whether the 7x7 feature map was averaged or flattened. Averaging halved
performance and prevented the model from fitting even the training set.

The pattern held across all twenty pretrained configurations: every GAP
configuration sat near chance (0.310 and 0.121), every Flatten
configuration cleared 0.42.

e1 failed for a different reason. Its 1x1 convolution squeezed 512
channels to 32 immediately before tanh dense layers, and the resulting
narrow bottleneck prevented training entirely - visible in its training
accuracy of 0.09 to 0.10 on four of five seeds, which is chance rather
than underfitting. The same failure appears in c2 and c3 (GAP feeding
tanh) and later in batch normalisation before the same layers. The
architecture is sensitive at the conv-to-dense boundary, and e1 hit
that sensitivity rather than testing what it was meant to test.

The methodological point: e1 was a single experiment intended to test
one hypothesis, and when it failed the hypothesis was discarded without
checking whether the experiment had failed for an unrelated reason. A
failed test of a hypothesis is only evidence against it if the test
itself was sound.
